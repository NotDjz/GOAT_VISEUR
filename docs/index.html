Ten presets on the keyboard

Ten crosshairs ready, each on its own key. You switch mid-match without opening a single window.

One page of settings

Screen, preset, shape, colors, preview and code: all of it under your eyes at once, with the hotkey modifier on a second tab. A setting that doesn't apply is grayed out rather than misleading.

Shortcuts

Mod is Ctrl+Shift by default. To change it, open the Shortcuts tab, press Change, and hold the combination you want: any mix of Ctrl, Alt, Shift and Win, at least two of them together. It applies straight away, and tells you if Windows refused a key because another application already holds it.

Two keys minimum is not a whim: a single modifier would turn Mod+S, Mod+Q and Mod+1–0 into global grabs, and the app would eat Save and Quit in every other application on your machine.

Ctrl+Alt is what Windows sends for AltGr. On a French AZERTY layout it takes @ ~ # { [ | ` \ off the keyboard, and the Shortcuts tab names the exact characters your own layout would lose before you keep it. Anything with Win mostly will not register, Windows reserving Win+1–0 and Win+Shift+S among others.
